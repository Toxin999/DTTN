# DetectiveNet — EN Style Guide (2000-2003 US internet)

Working doc for all English narrative text. Goal: text must read like it was
actually typed by American users in 2000-2003, not like modern text wearing a
retro costume. Players who lived that era will notice fakes within one line.

## 1. Setting quick facts

- Home access: dial-up still common (busy line, "get off the phone"), cable/DSL
  arriving. IM replaces the phone for teens. Long-distance charges are real.
- Platforms: AIM/ICQ/MSN, IRC, web forums (phpBB/vBulletin era), guestbooks,
  GeoCities/Angelfire/Tripod personal pages, WebRings, Yahoo/AskJeeves/early
  Google, Napster (dead by ~2002) then Kazaa.
- Hardware: CRTs, beige towers, CD-Rs, ZIP disks, floppy still around,
  disposable cameras + early digicams (640x480, orange date stamp), no
  smartphone, no wifi, no social media.
- Culture touchstones: Blockbuster, payphones, collect calls, burned mix CDs,
  yearbooks, mall arcades, landline etiquette.

## 2. Banned words (modern anachronisms)

Never use: DM, private message as verb "PM me"? (PM is OK on forums, not on
AIM), cringe, based, sus, ghosted, vibe check, lowkey/highkey, rizz, no cap,
lit, "delete this", socials, unfollow, influencer, streamer, Discord, Reddit
(2005+), YouTube (2005+), Facebook (2004+), Instagram, TikTok, Twitter,
smartphone, wifi, AirPods, Uber (2009+), Google Maps, "content creator",
emoji (ASCII emoticons only: :) ;) :( ).

## 3. Allowed era slang (use with restraint, not every line)

brb, bbl, afk, ttyl, l8r, g2g, kk, kthx, lol, rofl, lmao (sparing),
w00t, pwned, l33t/1337, n00b/newbie, flame/flamewar, troll (rare), bump,
sticky, sig, avatar, thread hijack, "pics or it didn't happen" (OK ~2003),
warez, "brb mom needs the phone", "are you online later?", "what's your SN?".

Typing texture: lowercase ramblers, ALL CAPS emphasis, missing apostrophes
(dont, cant, wont), "u" and "ur" in IM (NOT in forum posts from adults),
random capitalization, run-on sentences, double spaces after periods.

## 4. Per-character typing rules (identity clue — keep consistent)

Each named character gets a short card. Clues come from style differences.

Template:

    HANDLE:        river_kat
    Channels:      forum (full punctuation, lowercase), AIM (choppy),
                   IRC (no caps), guestbook (formal-ish)
    Habits:        trail/outdoors words; signs posts with "— k"
    Typos:         occasional "jsut"; never uses "u"
    NEVER:         !!!, ALL CAPS, emoji

Rules: 6-10 named characters; nobody shares another's habit signature; a
handle impersonating someone else must *importantly* differ in one habit
(that is a clue, and it must be documented in the beat sheet).

## 5. Good vs bad examples

Bad (modern):    "i'll just dm you the pic, it's kinda cringe"
Good (2002 AIM): "i'll send it when i get on later, brb phone"
Good (2002 forum): "I'll scan the photo tonight and post it in this thread.
                    Dial-up, so it might take a bit."

Bad:  "check my insta for pics"
Good: "photos are on my geocities page, link in sig"

Bad:  "sus"
Good: "sketchy" / "weird" / "something's off"

## 6. Research workflow

- Wayback Machine snapshots of 2000-2003 forums/AIM away-message archives;
  read real threads before writing a character's voice.
- Collect 20 lines of real-era text per character archetype and imitate the
  rhythm, not just the vocabulary.
- Keep a local scratch file of sourced phrases for the writer + editor.

## 7. Editor checklist (native speaker pass)

- [ ] No banned word appears (grep the list).
- [ ] Dated references are within 2000-2003 (tech, brands, slang).
- [ ] Each character matches their card; impersonation scene differs.
- [ ] Forum vs IM vs IRC textures differ (punctuation, caps, typos).
- [ ] Read aloud: sounds like a person, not a localization.
- [ ] No 2020s punctuation habits (no "—" spam in IM, no emoji, no lowercase
      aesthetic).
